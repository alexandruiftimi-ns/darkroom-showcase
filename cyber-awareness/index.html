    WHITE HAT Field guide · No. 01

    Stay one move ahead.

    The city runs on data, and so do the people hunting it. Here are the attacks aimed at you, and the moves that stop them, from people who break into systems for a living, with permission.

    Scroll to run the sim

    THREAT 01 Phishing

    It drives straight at you, looking normal.

    A parcel text. A bank alert. A boss in a hurry. Phishing wins on speed and panic.

    THREAT 01 Phishing · counter

    Don't take the link. Step over it.

    Open the app, or type the address yourself. A real company never needs you to use the link in the message.

    ▸ MOVE Check the real sender address, then report and delete.

    THREAT 02 Account takeover

    One reused password is a wall they can climb.

    When one site leaks, attackers try that password everywhere else. Same password, same result: they're in everywhere.

    ▸ MOVE A unique password per site, plus two-factor sign-in.

    THREAT 03 Data breaches

    Your details are already up here.

    Companies get breached. Emails, phone numbers and old passwords end up in lists traded online, and those lists are where every attack starts.

    ▸ MOVE Check if your email is in a known breach. Change every password it exposed.

    THREAT 04 Impersonation

    They jump the gap with a good story.

    A cloned voice. "Mum, it's my new number." A CEO who needs gift cards today. The story is the weapon.

    ▸ MOVE Hang up and call back on a number you already know.

    THREAT 05 Ransomware

    They chain your files and sell you the key.

    Ransomware encrypts everything it can reach, then asks for payment. Paying guarantees nothing.

    COUNTER Ransomware

    A backup is the blade.

    With a copy they can't reach, the lock means nothing: wipe, restore, move on. Updates keep most of it out in the first place.

    ▸ MOVE Turn on auto-updates. Keep one backup offline.

    THREAT 06 Surveillance

    Something is always watching.

    Data brokers, trackers, apps that ask for more than they need. Every location tag tells them a bit more about you.

    ▸ MOVE Audit app permissions. Think before you post where you are.

    THREAT 06 Stalkerware

    Take the eye out of your pocket.

    Fast battery drain, apps you didn't install, someone who knows things they shouldn't. Those are signs of stalkerware.

    ▸ MOVE Check which apps have admin or accessibility rights. If you feel unsafe, talk to a support line before removing anything.

    TRAINING 01 Spot the phish

    Five red flags are hiding in this email.
    Click each one you spot.

    Threat
    neutralised
    inbox / unread
    From
    NeoBank Security
    Subject

    We detected unusual sign-in activity on your account. To avoid permanent suspension, verify your identity immediately using the secure link below.

    → http://neobank.account-check.ru/login

    For your records, your statement is attached:

    NeoBank Security Team

    0/5 flags found
    1. Lookalike domain. The name says NeoBank, but the address is a random ".help" site. The address is what counts, not the name.
    2. Manufactured panic. A deadline and capital letters are there to stop you thinking.
    3. No name. Your real bank knows your name. "Dear customer" means a mass mailing.
    4. The link lies. Hover before you click: it goes to a different domain. Open your bank's app yourself instead.
    5. Double extension. ".pdf.exe" is a program dressed up as a document. Never open it.

    ▸ All five found. If you're unsure about a real email, contact the company through its official app or website, never through the email itself.

    PROTOCOL Six moves

    Six moves that stop most attacks on ordinary people.

    1. 01

      Turn on 2FA

      A code or key on top of your password. A stolen password alone stops working.

      → email, bank, socials first
    2. 02

      Use a password manager

      One strong master password; a unique random one for every site.

      → ends password reuse
    3. 03

      Update everything

      Updates close the holes attackers already know about. Turn on automatic updates.

      → phone, laptop, router
    4. 04

      Back up offline

      Three copies, two kinds of storage, one kept away from your computer.

      → the 3-2-1 rule
    5. 05

      Verify out of band

      Urgent money or password request? Call them back on a number you already know.

      → beats voice-clone scams
    6. 06

      Audit permissions

      Location, mic, contacts. Remove what an app doesn't need.

      → every few months
