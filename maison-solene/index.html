Salle I · N° 1 · Eau de Parfum

Lumière

after Turner

J. M. W. Turner (1775–1851)

Norham Castle, Sunrise, c. 1845

Oil on canvas · Tate, London

Turner painted the sun until only its warmth was left. Lumière does the same with jasmine: bergamot and neroli dissolve into a haze of orris and white musk, bright at first, then simply warm on the skin.

Salle II · N° 2 · Eau de Parfum

Ombre

after Whistler

James McNeill Whistler (1834–1903)

Nocturne in Black and Gold – The Falling Rocket, 1875

Oil on panel · Detroit Institute of Arts

A black night, a few sparks of gold falling slowly through it. Ombre is smoke and polished wood, close and unhurried: birch tar and leather over a deep base of oud, with saffron glinting through like the last of the fireworks.

L’Atelier · Rue de Valois, Paris

“A perfume is not worn.
It is met.”

Solène Arnaud, founder

Maison Solène was opened in a narrow workshop off the Palais-Royal, with one perfumer, one organ of raw materials and a belief that a scent should take its time. It still does. Each composition rests for twelve weeks before it is bottled, and nothing leaves the atelier until it has been worn for a full day.

We work in small batches and buy from the same growers each season: jasmine picked before dawn in Grasse, iris roots aged three years in Tuscany, oud from a single family in Laos. The flacons are blown in Normandy and finished by hand in Paris, the gold applied one line at a time.
